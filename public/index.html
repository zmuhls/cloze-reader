The cloze procedure was introduced in 1953 by Wilson Taylor, who adapted the Gestalt principle of closure to measure reading comprehension: delete words from a passage at regular intervals and ask readers to supply them from context. The same logic resurfaced decades later as masked language modeling, the training objective behind BERT and its successors. Both rest on the distributional hypothesis — the claim that words derive meaning from the contexts in which they appear. Cloze Reader sits at the intersection of these two histories, returning the fill-in-the-blank task to human readers at a moment when machines perform it at scale.

Each round presents a passage drawn from Project Gutenberg and removes one or more words, selected by an AI model rather than at fixed intervals. Your job is to read the surrounding context and infer the missing word. You can consult structured hints about part of speech, sentence role, or synonymy, but the answer is never given directly. Difficulty scales across two dimensions as you advance: the number of blanks per passage increases, and the vocabulary the model selects from shifts from common to challenging. Scores and streaks track your progress, and a leaderboard records the highest levels reached. No two sessions are identical — word selection uses non-zero temperature decoding, so the same passage can yield different blanks on different runs.

The app runs on Cloudflare Workers with a Hono backend. Passages stream from the Hugging Face Datasets API, and word selection, hint generation, and passage contextualization are each handled by separate calls to Google's Gemma-3-27B model via OpenRouter — the model never carries context between tasks, so selection cannot drift toward words the hint system handles fluently. The source archive is deliberate: Gutenberg texts are canonical pretraining data, already absorbed into LLM parameter weights and disaggregated from their narrative forms. Cloze Reader restores those flattened texts to the surface and asks human readers to engage them at a pace the model's low-latency engine cannot match. The project is open source at github.com/zmuhls/cloze-reader.
